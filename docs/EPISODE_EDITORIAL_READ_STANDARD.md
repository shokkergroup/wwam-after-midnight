# WWAM Episode Editorial Read Standard

This is the release standard for a public WWAM Show Wiki. It exists because a
timestamp database is not an episode recap, and a transcript fragment is not a
joke.

## The failure specimen

The original Christmas 2025 page exposed the recurring archive defects:

- The “30-second version” read like a list of indexed nouns.
- Keyword proximity invented segments that never existed.
- Several cards reused the same timestamp under different labels.
- Automatic-caption fragments were presented as finished copy.
- A character’s name was treated as proof that Mike or J performed the
  character.
- The page omitted the tier-list outcome, emotional turns, running bits and
  actual ending—the things a fan would remember.
- Evidence-system vocabulary leaked into visitor copy.

The public page must never publish those defects merely because the archive has
not received a complete editorial read. An unfinished show gets a useful,
source-linked subject index with no invented story, fake best-of shelf, guessed
Steve/Up In Ya verdict, or raw automatic-caption card. Gibberish is not a
placeholder.

## What “full-tape human editorial read” means

An editor listens to or reads the exact full upload, in order, and verifies its
duration and transcript identity. The resulting pack contains:

1. A headline that tells the night’s actual story.
2. A short deck with attitude, not metadata.
3. A conversational overview that explains why this episode is worth opening.
4. Chronological chapters covering the whole runtime.
5. An uncapped set of meaningful, playable highlights.
6. Episode-specific verdicts, rankings or other structured outcomes when the
   tape supports them.
7. A “Straight to Steve’s Asshole” selection grounded in a real negative take.
8. A “WWAM Up In Ya” selection grounded in a real deranged/funny detour.
9. Verified character performances, separated from casting talk, impressions
   discussed in the third person and simple name-drops.
10. Serious or community moments when the show earns them. The archive should
    not flatten a sincere conversation into a comedy clip.

## Voice

Write like a smart fan who watched the entire show and knows why the room
laughed. The copy can be profane, R-rated and irreverent because that is WWAM’s
actual register. It still must be readable.

Good:

> A dead stream crawls back online, Supergirl gets thrown into a burning diaper
> can, and Jaws nearly ends a friendship.

Bad:

> Superman // Casting // Bat-Field

Good:

> Thirty minutes at a barbecue and ugly effects earn Last Rites a one-way trip
> into Steve.

Bad:

> a fuck so i’m gonna walk out...

Do not:

- manufacture quotes;
- assign an automatic-caption line to Mike or J without diarized evidence;
- summarize a subject from its title alone;
- call a reference a character appearance;
- repeat a timestamp to make an episode look deeper;
- force every show to contain the same number of moments;
- expose schema, fingerprint, receipt, machine-state or review-queue language to
  visitors.

## Depth floors, never caps

These are rejection floors for suspiciously thin work:

| Runtime | Minimum story chapters | Minimum highlights |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Under 1 hour | 3 | 4 |
| 1–2 hours | 5 | 6 |
| 2–3 hours | 8 | 10 |
| 3+ hours | 10 | 15 |

A four-hour show with 44 memorable moments keeps all 44. A quiet show with 15
keeps 15. The tape decides.

## Character truth

Use three distinct concepts:

- **Performance:** Mike or J audibly becomes Loomis, Challis, Slenderman,
  Feldman or another persona.
- **Reference/callback:** the character is discussed, quoted, cast or named.
- **Unverified:** captions suggest a possible bit but the moment has not been
  heard by an editor.

Only the first belongs in the playable character-performance count.

## Release gate

Before a human pack ships:

- source ID, duration and caption hash match the exact upload;
- every play window is bounded by that duration;
- story and highlight windows are unique;
- labels and copy are readable in the browser;
- all chapters and highlights expand;
- at least one highlight is played in-page to verify the exact source and start
  time;
- the page contains no “Show Wiki unavailable” failure;
- unfinished summaries expose only clean topic doors, never internal
  best-moment or signature-bit candidates;
- editorial truth, recap, semantics, copy, UI and source-dossier tests pass.

The archive-wide progressive release may contain unfinished shows only when
those pages use the safe summary/index path. The completion audit remains red
until every ready show has a full-tape human pack.
